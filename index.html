Target

Five lanes, run in parallel and weighted by how fast each one actually pays. Lanes A through C close in weeks to a few months. D and E close in quarters. The ordering below reflects PCC-IT's own stated focus: grow higher education and state and local, hold K-12 opportunistically post-ESSER.

Lane A · California state agencies

CYCLE 30–90 DAYS · FASTEST LEGAL CLOSE

California's SB/DVBE Option lets any state department award a contract from $5,000.01 to $249,999.99 — goods, services or IT — to a certified small business with two quotes and no advertising. PCC-IT is DGS-certified, and departments carry participation goals they are measured against.

This is the shortest legal path from cold to closed anywhere in the territory, and almost nobody works it properly. Entry is not the CIO — it is the department's Small Business Advocate, a published, named person whose job is to take the meeting.

Why we win hereCertification plus real California past performance at Franchise Tax Board, DGS and LA County. CDW and SHI cannot use this door.

Lane B · Federal via SEWP VI

OPENS 1 NOV · HIGHEST CEILING

PCC-IT holds SEWP VI as a direct prime — no partnering, no distributor letter of authorization. The vehicle is expected to carry heavy RFQ volume from go-live.

That volume is competed among hundreds of holders, which compresses margin and makes cold response a poor use of a quarter. Treat the feed as market intelligence, not a work queue: it shows which agencies buy what, so we can be in front of the next requirement before it is written. Once a solicitation issues, contact is restricted to the contracting officer — so October is for capability briefings and November is for orders.

DoorAgency OSDBU offices, whose statutory job is to meet small businesses. DOJ components first, on the strength of DEA past performance.

Lane C · Higher ed, cities & counties

CYCLE 6–16 WEEKS · STATED GROWTH PRIORITY

OMNIA, NCPA and Equalis members purchase off the awarded contract directly. Universities and community college districts buy at scale, refresh on predictable cycles, and carry research-computing and security budgets that K-12 no longer has. Work it through one free signal instead of broad outreach: E-Rate Form 470 filings are public and show exactly which districts have funded network projects and who to call. A district with an active 470 has money. One without probably does not.

The single highest-yield move in this plan

Small Business Advocates and OSDBU officers are measured on how many small businesses they meet. A capability-briefing request is not an interruption — it helps them hit a number, and it ends with an internal introduction to the person who actually buys. Twenty of those requests in the first two weeks is the difference between three meetings and eighteen.

CRM discipline

Every interaction logged in HubSpot the same day — deal progress, issues, costs, registration, proposals, quotes, site visits, meetings, calls, BOM discussions, pricing and next steps. Referrals called within 24 hours and emailed within 24–48 if no response, then re-contacted every two to three days until they respond or decline. Accounts registered before pursuit so no two reps work the same buyer. Clean records are not administrative overhead here — they are the condition on getting paid.

Discovery

In public sector, the procurement path is as much a qualifier as the pain. A funded need with no legal route to purchase is not an opportunity — it is a year of work.

Environment & scale

  • Users, sites, endpoints, and who supports them today
  • Refresh cycle and where you are in it
  • Which manufacturers you are standardized on
  • What runs on-premise versus cloud, and why

Buying path

  • How do you buy technology today — bid, co-op, state contract?
  • Which cooperative contracts are you a member of?
  • What is your threshold for a formal solicitation?
  • Who signs at $50K, and who signs at $500K?

Pain & timing

  • What would you fix first if the money appeared tomorrow?
  • What happens if it does not get done this fiscal year?
  • Has anyone tried before — what stopped it?
  • When does your fiscal year end, and what is unspent?

AI readiness & risk

  • What AI initiatives are underway, and who governs them?
  • Where does the data live, and is it clean enough?
  • What does your security review require of a new vendor?
  • Any compliance frame — CMMC, NIST, HIPAA, PCI, FERPA?

The Wedge

Nobody buys a seven-figure AI program from a rep they met last week. But an honest AI conversation surfaces the same four things every time: the data is not clean, the network cannot carry it, security will not approve it, and there is not enough compute. Those four findings are a bill of materials.

The AI Readiness Assessment

A structured 90-minute working session. Outputs: a use-case shortlist, a data readiness score, an infrastructure gap list, a security gap list, and a rough cost model. Offered at no cost, or at a nominal fee where that qualifies harder.

It is three things at once — a reason to meet that is not a pitch, a qualification tool, and a bill-of-materials generator. Every output maps to a PCC-IT line item, and every line item sits on a vehicle the buyer can already purchase from.

Why it is credible coming from me

Every reseller rep in the country is saying the word AI this year. Very few have deployed anything. I have built and shipped conversational AI agents in production for paying clients, along with CRM-integrated lead pipelines, workflow automation and performance dashboards.

That makes one sentence true and unusual: "It is never the model that breaks. It is the data, the network, and the security review." A buyer who has lived through a stalled pilot recognizes that immediately.

The expansion ladder

One project → the department → the agency or campus → the sister sites → the managed services wrap. A single $85,000 switch refresh at one campus is not interesting on its own. The same refresh across eleven campuses, wrapped in managed security with a three-year term, is the shape of the business. Every first order is scoped with the second one in mind, and the question "where else does this exist?" is asked on the delivery call, not six months later.

Procurement rules that govern this territory

Public-sector selling has a rulebook, and breaking it does not cost a deal — it costs an agency, permanently. These are the ones that shape day-to-day behavior.

California SB/DVBE Option

State departments may award contracts from $5,000.01 to $249,999.99 — goods, services or IT — to certified small businesses with a minimum of two responsive quotes from certified firms and no advertising required. Best value governs, and departments are directed to expand the pool of certified firms they use. PCC-IT's DGS certification is the ticket; the Small Business Advocate is the door.

Cooperative purchasing

A member agency may purchase off a contract competitively awarded by a lead agency, without running its own solicitation. That is the mechanism behind PEPPM, OMNIA, NCPA and Equalis. The seller's job is to confirm membership, name the contract number on the quote, and make the requisition easy for a purchasing agent who has never bought from us before.

Contracting officer contact

Once a solicitation is issued, the contracting officer is the only authorized contact. Calling a program manager or technical lead during an open procurement can disqualify the bid and damage the relationship permanently. All relationship work happens before issuance — which is precisely why the pre-positioning calendar matters more than the RFQ feed.

Gifts, meals and hospitality

Many agencies prohibit public employees from accepting anything of value, including a cup of coffee. Limits vary by state, agency and locality. The working rule: offer nothing, ask first, and let them pay their own way. The commercial-sector reflex of picking up lunch is a liability here.

Verify before quoting

Contract vehicles expire, lapse and get renumbered. Quoting an expired vehicle to a contracting officer ends credibility with that agency for good. Every vehicle referenced in a quote gets confirmed current that quarter — number, expiration date, and scope — before it appears in writing to a customer.

Fiscal-year timing

Federal years close 30 September. Most states and districts close 30 June. Calendar-year commercial buyers plan in the fall and release in January. Those three clocks are the single biggest driver of when a known need becomes a funded order, and the pipeline is sequenced against them rather than against our own month-end.
